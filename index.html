Featured Work

Insulin Pens: A Competitive Landscape

Scope: conventional and connected insulin-pen platforms across 5 companies — independent research, verified against primary/manufacturer sources.

Cost Architecture: What Changes with a Smart Pen?

Mechanical / delivery
Core injection mechanism remains the main physical cost base; smart designs add complexity rather than replacing the delivery function.
Electronics & connectivity
Smart pens add electronics, memory/connectivity and related components conventional pens don't require.
Software & data layer
Connected products add apps, data capture, integration and ongoing software support beyond the physical pen.
Validation & lifecycle
Connected systems require broader device + software validation and added support/maintenance requirements.

The smart-pen premium is better understood as a cost architecture than a single hardware markup — electronics, software/data, and validation add layers, while reusable vs. disposable design changes the replacement economics.

2–3 Year Outlook

A "COEXISTENCE" MARKET, NOT A TAKEOVER

Working hypothesis: smart pens expand over the next 2–3 years, but conventional pens remain the scale segment. Drivers: automated dose capture, digital diabetes management, CGM/app integration. Barriers: affordability, reimbursement, device complexity, digital-ecosystem adoption.

[1]
This is a coexistence market, not a takeover.
Conventional pens retain the accessibility and scale proposition; smart pens differentiate where connectivity and clinical utility justify added complexity.
[2]
Device innovation becomes more valuable when paired with commercial scale.
embecta's 2026 Owen Mumford acquisition combines device-design/manufacturing capability with global commercial infrastructure — pairing engineering with reach.
[3]
Device and software expertise can create advantage without owning the drug.
Medtronic's InPen combines automated dose logging, dose calculation, and CGM-enabled functionality — showing how the delivery interface itself can differentiate a diabetes platform.
[4]
Owning both drug and device is a trade-off, not a pure win.
Pharma ownership can strengthen control of the patient ecosystem, but external partnerships remain important for connecting insulin dosing with CGM, apps, and digital care.
